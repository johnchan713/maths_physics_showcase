## 2. Real Gevrey exponential, using the actual value gate

Normalize finite (r,k) jets by r!k!, then divide by (r!k!)².
For each multivariate composition, the product of the constituent
factorials squared is at most the factorial of the final multiindex
squared, coordinate by coordinate. Consequently the positive-degree
transformed increments in D are bounded by the analytic MAJORANT

    T[(1-Rx)^-1(1-Bz)^-1-1].

Only this majorant is analytic; no slow Taylor convergence through a flat
cutoff is asserted. Evaluate at x=1/[8R(1+T)], z=1/[8B(1+T)]. Writing
u=1/[8(1+T)]<=1/8, its value S satisfies

    S=T[(1-u)^-2-1] <=(128/49)Tu <=16/49<1,
    S<=15T/49<T.

The positive series of the exponent has value below exp(1)<3. At each
real base, exp(plus/minus D0)<=exp(V0/N)<3, or <=exp(1)<3 at equality.
Cauchy coefficient bounds and the finite real chain rule therefore give

    |partial_y^r partial_eta^k exp(plus/minus D)|
       <=9 Ly^r Leta^k(r!k!)^3,
    Ly=8R(1+T), Leta=8B(1+T).                          (3)

For the DIFFERENCE, at degree zero use |exp(plus/minus D0)-1|<=3V0/N.
At positive degrees use exp(plus/minus D0)[exp(S)-1]<=9S<=9T.
The resulting uniform cap is

    |partial_y^r partial_eta^k [exp(plus/minus D)-1]|
       <=9 max(V0,M)/N * Ly^r Leta^k(r!k!)^3.           (4)

Thus high mixed norm constants are not exponentiated as though they
were real value bounds. Radial bases still carry N; (4) is not radial
O(1/N) smallness. The original sharper low-order acceptance bounds remain.
